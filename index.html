SCENARIO CONTEXT

The fog of misinformation

In Mistvale, messages can arrive late or change between departments. Citizens need one clear, official source.

The ethical problem is not just a technical error. Conflicting messages can damage trust and harm people who depend on public information.

This is a fictional teaching scenario from the Beacon proposal.

02 / PEOPLE FIRST

Who is affected?

Ethical decisions consider people, not only processes. Adjust the impact level for each group.

♧   CitizensNeed reliable information to plan their lives.
▦   Government departmentsNeed one shared, correct message.
⌂   VillagesMay receive important updates late.
Privacy rule: personal names should only be recorded with consent.
WHY THIS MATTERS

Impact is not equal

A late or incorrect message can hurt some groups more than others. Beacon makes these differences visible before a decision is made.

Priority: protect people with the highest impact, especially those who receive information late.
03 / ETHICAL ANALYSIS

Two tests. One responsible choice.

Answer each question. Beacon will use your answers to suggest a decision. The final choice always stays human.

TEST 01 · UTILITARIANISM
Will this message help most people?
Think about the results. Does sharing it now create more benefits than harm?
TEST 02 · KANTIAN ETHICS
Is this message honest and respectful?
Could everyone follow this rule? Does the message avoid misleading people?

How the decision works

Utilitarianism
Choose an action that helps the greatest number of people.

Kantian ethics
Tell the truth and respect people, even when a shortcut seems easier.

If the tests disagree, Beacon does not make the final choice. It asks the Royal Ethical Council to review the case.

Demo decision rules are simplified for learning; this is not an automated legal or factual review.

04 / DECISION CENTER

Compare three possible decisions and review Beacon's recommendation.

AWAITING ANSWERS
OPTION A

Publish now

Fast, but risky if the facts are not clear.

OPTION B

Verify and revise

Check the facts, agree on one message, then publish.

OPTION C

Hold the message

Do not publish until serious problems are resolved.

Practical action plan

  1. Confirm the correct information with both departments.
  2. Write one clear, official message.
  3. Add the date and responsible department.
  4. Ask the Queen or an authorized officer to approve.
  5. Publish and save the final record.

Ethical justification

Checking the facts first protects citizens from confusion and respects their right to honest information.

Human oversight: Beacon recommends. The Queen or authorized officer decides.

One important limitation

Beacon depends on correct information entered by people. It cannot confirm real-world facts on its own.

One future improvement

Connect Beacon to verified government data so it can automatically flag conflicting facts.

The idea behind Beacon.

An interactive learning prototype created for the Royal Ethical Council of the Kingdom of Mistvale.

Project background

In the fictional Kingdom of Mistvale, different government departments share conflicting information. Citizens are confused, and trust is at risk. Beacon provides a guided ethical review before messages are shared.

How to present it live

1. Load the demo case.
2. Show the stakeholders.
3. Answer the two ethical tests.
4. Compare three options.
5. Request royal approval.
6. Open the Royal Record.
